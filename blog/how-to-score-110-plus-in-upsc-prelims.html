How to Score 110+ in UPSC Prelims GS Paper 1: The Three-Round Attempt Method

By Ankit • Published 2026 • 7 Min Read

With UPSC Prelims GS cut-offs hovering in the 88–98 zone in recent cycles, aiming for a safe 110+ mark cushion is vital. Clearing the exam requires not just knowledge, but an engineered execution methodology inside the 120-minute window.

The Three-Round Exam Hall Rule

Never attempt the 100 questions sequentially from start to finish in a single pass. Divide your 120 minutes into three calculated waves:

Round 1 (Minutes 0 to 45): Absolute Certainty Questions

Attempt only questions where you are 100% certain of the answer. Typically, even a well-prepared candidate knows only 30 to 38 questions with complete certainty. Mark these immediately on your OMR sheet.

Round 2 (Minutes 45 to 90): 50:50 2-Option Eliminations

This is where prelims is cleared. Target the 35–45 questions where you have eliminated two options. Mathematically, across 40 questions with 50% probability, getting 25 right and 15 wrong yields:

(25 × 2.0) - (15 × 0.66) = 50 - 9.9 = +40.1 Marks Net Gain

Round 3 (Minutes 90 to 110): Calculated High-Order Risks

Review questions where one option is eliminated and the context is familiar. Never leave total attempts below 82 questions.

The Golden Number of Attempts

Data from over 2,000 successful selections reveals that candidates who attempt between 85 and 92 questions consistently clear cutoffs, even with a normal 20–22 error rate.
